## Context

FIRST Global brings together national teams in an international robotics environment where engineering decisions must survive real match conditions, inspection requirements, schedule pressure and rapidly changing strategy.

The 2026 competition cycle concluded on **10 October 2026**. My role was **Coach — Team India**.

## Coaching contribution

The coaching role focused on creating a disciplined engineering and competition-preparation process rather than making isolated technical decisions for the team.

Areas of contribution included:

- structured engineering reviews and decision discussions;
- competition and match-strategy development;
- documentation and communication preparation;
- readiness tracking across technical and competition requirements; and
- helping the team connect design choices with field performance.

## Engineering architecture

The competition robot was developed as an integrated set of mechanical, electrical and control subsystems rather than as a collection of independent mechanisms.

### Chassis and drivetrain

The base architecture provided the structural platform for the robot while supporting drivetrain components, electronics and the mechanisms required for game interaction.

### Intake

The intake mechanism was designed to acquire game elements from the field and transfer them into the robot's internal handling system.

### Transfer system

A dedicated transfer mechanism managed the movement of game elements between acquisition and scoring stages. Its design had to consider alignment, spacing, continuous movement and integration with adjacent mechanisms.

### Shooter

The scoring mechanism used a motor-driven wheel arrangement designed around controlled transfer of energy to the game element. Mechanical layout, wheel spacing and actuation had to remain consistent with the overall robot geometry.

### Climbing mechanism

The climbing subsystem introduced additional structural and loading considerations. The mechanism needed to generate sufficient mechanical action while remaining integrated with the robot frame and avoiding unnecessary interference with other subsystems.

### Electrical architecture

The robot used the REV Robotics control ecosystem, with power and motor connections distributed between the Control Hub and Expansion Hub. Electrical architecture was documented separately so that subsystem connections could be inspected, traced and serviced systematically.

### Driver interface

Controller mapping was treated as part of the engineering system rather than an afterthought. Driver controls were documented so that robot functions could be operated consistently during practice and competition.

## Design Engineering connection

The programme also created opportunities for learners from REVA University's School of Design Engineering to engage with international competition practice through match analysis, strategy, engineering documentation and visual communication.

This connection between academic learning and a live competition ecosystem reflects a broader objective of my work: creating environments where students can observe engineering decisions under authentic constraints.
